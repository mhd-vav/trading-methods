# Elliott Wave Theory

Elliott Wave Theory (EWT) is a form of technical analysis that holds markets move in repetitive, predictable wave patterns driven by mass investor psychology — alternating cycles of optimism and pessimism. Developed by Ralph Nelson Elliott in the 1930s, it proposes that trends unfold in a **5-wave motive sequence** (in the direction of the larger trend) followed by a **3-wave corrective sequence** (against it), and that this 5+3 structure is **fractal** — the same pattern appears at every degree of trend, from centuries to minutes.

EWT is the most ambitious of the chart-based methods: it aims not just to read current structure but to *forecast* future price movement by identifying where in the wave count the market currently sits. Its power and its controversy both stem from this ambition — when the count is right it frames large moves beautifully; when ambiguous, it is famously subject to retrospective re-counting.

## Core Philosophy — Crowd Psychology in Waves

Elliott's thesis: investor psychology oscillates between optimism and pessimism in recognizable patterns, and these patterns produce waves. The pattern is not random — it follows rules derived from the rhythm of crowd behavior. Because the same psychological dynamics operate at all timescales, the wave structure is **self-similar (fractal)**: a 5-wave move on the daily chart contains 5-wave sub-structures on the hourly chart, which contain 5-wave structures on the 5-minute chart.

The key insight for traders: trends are not linear but progress in a 5-step motive pattern, and corrections retrace in a 3-step pattern. Knowing the current wave position tells you both the likely next move *and* how far it may extend.

## The Motive Sequence — 5 Waves

In an uptrend, the motive phase consists of five waves labeled 1-2-3-4-5:

- **Waves 1, 3, 5** are *impulse* waves — they move in the direction of the larger trend.
- **Waves 2, 4** are *corrective* waves — they move against the trend (pullbacks).

The pattern: 1 up → 2 down (retrace) → 3 up (usually the longest/strongest) → 4 down (retrace) → 5 up. Net result: a directional advance with two counter-trend pullbacks.

### The Three Hard Rules (non-negotiable)

A valid Elliott count must satisfy these; violating any invalidates the count:

1. **Wave 2 never retraces more than 100% of Wave 1.** If it does, the "Wave 1" was mislabeled. Wave 2's bottom stays above Wave 1's origin.
2. **Wave 3 is never the shortest of the three impulse waves (1, 3, 5).** It is usually the longest and most dynamic, but even if not, it cannot be the shortest.
3. **Wave 4 does not overlap the price territory of Wave 1.** Wave 4's low stays above Wave 1's high (in an uptrend). Overlap means the count is wrong (or it's a corrective pattern, not motive).

These rules are what make EWT falsifiable and give it structure. The famous "alternate" guideline: **Wave 2 and Wave 4 tend to alternate in character** — if Wave 2 was a sharp correction, Wave 4 is usually a sideways/complex one, and vice versa.

### Wave Personalities

- **Wave 1:** the start of a new trend, often unrecognized; skepticism is high (the prior trend's adherents still dominate). Frequently a short, tentative move.
- **Wave 2:** a pullback that retraces much of Wave 1 — but not all (Rule 1). Bears believe the old trend is resuming; this is where the final weak hands are shaken out.
- **Wave 3:** the powerful, extended move. The crowd now recognizes the trend; news and sentiment align; volume and momentum peak. Usually the longest and most profitable to trade. Often 1.618× Wave 1 (Fibonacci).
- **Wave 4:** a consolidative, often sideways/complex correction. Less severe than Wave 2 (alternation). The market digests Wave 3's gains.
- **Wave 5:** the final impulse — often driven by euphoria/latecomers. Frequently weaker than Wave 3 (momentum divergence common here). The top/bottom that ends the motive phase.

## The Corrective Sequence — 3 Waves (A-B-C)

After the 5-wave motive move, a 3-wave correction labeled A-B-C retraces part of it:

- **Wave A:** the first leg against the trend. Often misread as a routine pullback.
- **Wave B:** a counter-rally (in a bearish correction) that traps bulls — a "fake" resumption of the prior trend.
- **Wave C:** the strong leg down that completes the correction, often equal to Wave A or Fibonacci-related.

The full cycle is 5 waves up + 3 waves down = 8 waves, then the next cycle begins at a higher degree.

### Corrective Patterns

Corrections are more varied and complex than impulses. The three main families:

- **Zigzag (5-3-5):** a sharp A-B-C where A and C are impulses, B is a smaller correction. Deep, fast retracements.
- **Flat (3-3-5):** A and B are 3-wave moves, C is a 5-wave impulse; overall shallower, sideways. Variants: regular, expanded (B exceeds Wave 5's high), running.
- **Triangle (3-3-3-3-3):** a contracting or expanding sideways triangle of five 3-wave legs (a-b-c-d-e). Often appears as Wave 4 or Wave B. Breakouts from triangles resolve in the trend direction.

Complex corrections combine these (e.g., a zigzag-flat "double three" joined by an X wave).

## Fibonacci Relationships

EWT is tightly bound to Fibonacci ratios, which quantify wave projections:

- **Wave 2** typically retraces **50% or 61.8%** of Wave 1.
- **Wave 3** is often **1.618×** Wave 1 (and can extend to 2.618×).
- **Wave 4** typically retraces **38.2%** of Wave 3 (shallower than Wave 2 by alternation).
- **Wave 5** is often equal to Wave 1 (1.0×) or **0.618×** Wave 3.
- **Wave C** is often equal to Wave A (1.0×) or **1.618×** Wave A.
- In triangles, adjacent legs relate by ~0.618.

These ratios give price targets and invalidation levels — the forecasting engine of EWT.

## Fractality and Degrees

Waves nest at multiple "degrees" (Grand Supercycle down to Minuette). A Wave 3 of Primary degree contains within it a full 5-3-5-3-5 of Cycle degree, which contains... down to intraday. Traders typically work 2–3 degrees: a higher-degree count for context/bias and a lower-degree count for entry. The fractality means the *same rules and ratios apply at every scale* — a beautiful but demanding property, because the count must be consistent across degrees.

## Trading With Elliott Wave

1. **Identify the larger-degree trend and current wave position.** Is the market in a motive 1-5 or a corrective A-B-C? Where within it?
2. **Trade Wave 3 (highest probability).** Enter on the confirmation that Wave 2 has ended (Wave 2 held above Wave 1, momentum turns up) and ride the strongest leg. Fibonacci 1.618 projection gives the target.
3. **Trade Wave 5 (with caution).** Enter after Wave 4 completes (Rule 3 holds, alternation satisfied); target Wave 5 = Wave 1 or 0.618× Wave 3. Watch for momentum divergence (Wave 5 often weaker).
4. **Trade the correction (C wave).** After a 5-wave move completes, position for the A-B-C. Wave C (equal to A or 1.618×A) is the tradeable leg.
5. **Use invalidation levels.** Each wave's rules define where the count is wrong — stop there. EWT's discipline is its rules; a count that survives rule-checks has objective invalidation.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Subjectivity and re-counting.** The biggest criticism. Live counts are often ambiguous; it's tempting to relabel after the fact ("the count was wrong, here's the real one"). Mitigate with strict rules and pre-committed invalidation levels.
- **Over-counting in real time.** Forcing a count onto choppy, unclear action produces false confidence. Sometimes the honest read is "unclear — stand aside."
- **Ignoring the rules.** Labeling an overlap as Wave 4 or letting Wave 3 be shortest to fit a desired count destroys the method's edge.
- **Neglecting higher degree.** Trading a lower-degree count against the higher-degree trend is low-probability.
- **Treating projections as certainties.** Fibonacci targets are *probable* zones, not guarantees. Use them with stops and confirmation.

## Relationship to Other Methods

- **Fibonacci Trading:** EWT is the primary consumer of Fibonacci ratios in forecasting. See [Fibonacci Trading](fibonacci-trading).
- **Price Action / Structure:** EWT is a structured form of price-action analysis (swings, impulse/correction). See [Price Action Trading](price-action).
- **Wyckoff / SMC:** Different models of the same phenomena — EWT's Wave 2 shakeout ≈ Wyckoff Spring / SMC liquidity sweep. See [Wyckoff Method](wyckoff-method).
- **MACD / Momentum:** Wave 3 = momentum peak; Wave 5 momentum divergence is a classic reversal warning. Indicators confirm wave structure.

## Why It Matters (and Its Limits)

Elliott Wave is unmatched for *context* — it frames where a market is in its larger cycle and what the probable next move is. Its fractal/Fibonacci structure gives genuine forecasting ability that pure price-action lacks. Its limitation is subjectivity: real-time counting is hard, and the method rewards extensive practice. Practitioners who internalize the rules and the discipline of invalidation gain a powerful roadmap; those who bend counts to their bias gain confirmation bias. Used as a *context and targeting* layer (combined with price-action entries and other methods for confirmation), EWT is among the most insightful chart frameworks.
