# Volume Spread Analysis (VSA)

Volume Spread Analysis (VSA) is a bar-by-bar price-and-volume reading method developed by Tom Williams, a former syndicate trader, who distilled Richard Wyckoff's early-1900s principles into a focused, practical discipline. Where [Wyckoff](wyckoff-method) gives a macro phase model (accumulation/markup/distribution/markdown), VSA zooms to the **single bar**: it examines the relationship between the **spread** (the bar's high-to-low range), the **close** (where the bar finished), and the **volume** (the effort behind the bar) to infer whether professional/smart money is buying or selling.

Its central question, applied to every bar: *Was the effort (volume) rewarded by the result (price spread and close)?* Agreement confirms the move; divergence warns of professional activity against the apparent direction — the precursor to a reversal. VSA is essentially Wyckoff's Law of Effort vs. Result operationalized bar by bar.

## Core Philosophy — Effort vs. Result, Bar by Bar

VSA assumes the market is driven by "professional" operators (institutions, syndicates) whose large orders leave traces in volume. Retail volume is small and diffuse; professional volume is large and concentrated. By reading whether volume is confirming or contradicting price action, the VSA trader infers the professionals' stance.

Two intertwined reads define every bar:

1. **The Spread (Result):** the bar's range (high − low) and, crucially, where it closed within that range. A wide spread closing near the high = buyers controlled the bar. A wide spread closing near the low = sellers controlled it.
2. **The Volume (Effort):** how much traded. High volume = significant effort/interest; low volume = lack of interest.

The analysis: **does the volume match the spread?**
- High volume + wide spread in the trend direction = genuine, confirmed move (professionals participating).
- High volume + *narrow* spread = effort with no result → professionals are absorbing the other side → potential reversal.
- Low volume + wide spread = move without effort → unsustainable, likely to retrace.
- Low volume + narrow spread = no interest → noise.

## Background Principles (from Wyckoff)

VSA inherits Wyckoff's three laws, applying them at the bar level:

- **Supply and Demand:** volume reveals which force dominates each bar.
- **Cause and Effect:** accumulation/distribution ranges (cause) precede markups/markdowns (effect) — VSA bar reads locate where within a range the cause is building.
- **Effort vs. Result:** the literal engine of VSA — volume (effort) vs. spread (result) agreement/divergence.

VSA also assumes a background context: it's most powerful when reading bars *within* a trading range or at the extremes of a move, where professional absorption/distribution is happening. A VSA signal in a vacuum is weak; in context (after a downtrend, at support) it's strong.

## The Core Bar Reads (VSA Signals)

### No Demand

A narrow-spread up-bar on **low volume** (often with an up-close) after a down-move or in a range. Interpretation: the rally had no professional buying behind it — "no demand." If the market is in a downtrend or distribution, no demand confirms sellers are still in control; the up-bar is a feeble rally that will be sold. A no-demand bar is a *sell* signal (or a warning to exit longs).

Key: low volume on an up-bar = professionals aren't interested in buying higher. The public may be bidding, but without professional support the move fails.

### No Supply

The mirror: a narrow-spread down-bar on **low volume** after an up-move or at support. Interpretation: the decline had no professional selling behind it — "no supply." Sellers are absent; the dip is shallow and unforced. In an uptrend or accumulation, no supply confirms buyers are still in control; a no-supply bar is a *buy* signal (or a warning to exit shorts).

### Effort vs. Result Divergence (The Reversal Signal)

The signature VSA setup:
- **High volume on a down-bar with a narrow spread** (often a long lower wick, closing off the low) at the bottom of a decline. Interpretation: huge effort to push price down (high volume), but the spread is narrow and price closed up off the low — the effort produced no downward result. Professionals were *absorbing* the selling (buying into the panic). This is strong evidence of accumulation and an impending up-move. (This is the bar-level signature of a Wyckoff Spring.)
- **High volume on an up-bar with a narrow spread** (long upper wick, closing off the high) at the top of a rally. Effort to push up, no result → professionals distributing into the buying → impending down-move. (Bar-level Wyckoff UTAD.)

### Stopping Volume

A high-volume down-bar (wide spread, closing near the low) that *stops* a downtrend by exhausting supply — the climactic volume signals selling pressure has spent itself (Wyckoff's Selling Climax at the bar level). Follow-through should dry up; the next bars reveal whether demand has taken over.

### Upthrust / Shakeout

A bar (or brief move) that spikes above resistance on volume then closes back below — a false breakout that traps buyers and shakes out sellers. The high volume + failure to hold = distribution. The VSA trader shorts the upthrust.

### Squat Bar

A bar with a narrow spread and high volume — effort without movement in either direction. A squat at a market turning point (after a trend) signals professional two-sided accumulation/distribution and an imminent breakout in the direction volume later confirms. Squats are tension: the market coiled.

### Pushing Through Supply/Demand (Confirmation)

A wide-spread up-bar on **increasing** volume that breaks through prior resistance (supply) and closes near the high. This is genuine demand overcoming supply — a confirmed breakout (Wyckoff SOS at bar level). The volume must increase on the breakout; a low-volume break is suspect (no demand).

## Reading the Sequence

VSA is contextual and sequential, not single-bar pattern-matching:

1. **Establish background:** What's the larger trend/range? Are we after a long decline (look for accumulation signals) or a long rally (distribution)?
2. **Read effort/result at key points:** At support after a decline, look for high-volume narrow-spread down-bars (stopping volume, absorption) or no-supply bars on dips. At resistance after a rally, look for high-volume narrow-spread up-bars (distribution) or no-demand on rallies.
3. **Confirm with follow-through:** A single divergent bar is a warning; the next 1–2 bars confirm. After a stopping-volume bar, look for no-supply (drying selling) then a push up on increased volume.
4. **Enter on confirmation:** after the no-supply/follow-through, with a stop beyond the absorption bar's extreme.

## The Volume Bar — Where It Matters

VSA cares about volume *relative to recent average*. A bar's volume is "high" or "low" against its rolling mean (e.g., 20-bar average volume). The absolute number is market-dependent; the relative reading is universal. Two volume bars are often compared: the current bar's volume vs. the prior two (as in order-block strength). Climactic volume is volume far exceeding the average — the exhaustion signature.

## Risk Management

- **Stops:** beyond the absorption/divergence bar's extreme. If a stopping-volume bar's low is violated on a close, the absorption thesis is wrong.
- **Targets:** the next opposing structure (prior high/low, range edge) or the start of the cause (Wyckoff projection). VSA entries at range extremes often offer high R:R because the divergence marks the turn.
- **Confirmation over anticipation:** entering on a single divergent bar is anticipation; waiting for the confirming no-supply/no-demand + follow-through raises hit rate.
- **Context filter:** only act on VSA signals that agree with background (accumulation signals after declines, distribution after rallies).

## Common Pitfalls

- **Reading bars in isolation.** A no-demand bar mid-range is meaningless. VSA needs background context (trend/range position) to interpret.
- **Ignoring the spread/close.** Volume alone is ambiguous; it must be read with the spread and the close position. High volume up = very different if it closed near the high (demand) vs. near the low (absorbed demand).
- **Confusing climactic volume with continuation.** A huge-volume wide bar in the trend direction *can* be the final exhaustion (climax), not confirmation. Context (after a long move = climax; early in a move = confirmation) decides.
- **Trading every divergence.** Divergences are warnings, not automatic entries. Many resolve into continuation. Wait for confirmation.
- **Low-volume instruments.** VSA needs reliable volume data; spot FX (no centralized volume) and illiquid stocks give misleading readings. Use futures volume or volume proxies where real volume is unavailable.

## Relationship to Other Methods

- **Wyckoff:** VSA is the bar-level distillation of Wyckoff's effort-vs-result law. A Wyckoff spring manifests as a VSA stopping-volume/absorption bar; a Wyckoff SOS as a VSA push-through-supply. See [Wyckoff Method](wyckoff-method).
- **Price Action:** VSA adds the volume dimension to price-action bar reading. See [Price Action Trading](price-action).
- **Market/Volume Profile:** Profile aggregates volume by *price level* (where); VSA reads volume by *bar/time* (when and how). Complementary views of the same volume data. See [Market Profile / Volume Profile](market-profile).
- **SMC/ICT:** SMC infers institutional action from price structure; VSA reads it from volume. A liquidity sweep with high-volume absorption is far higher conviction than price alone. See [Smart Money Concepts / ICT](smc-ict).
- **Weis Wave:** A VSA-related tool (David Weis) that cumulates volume in the trend direction into "waves," visualizing effort/result over multi-bar swings. The srl indicators implement Weis Wave.

## Why It's Valuable

Volume is the only truly leading data in technical analysis — it reflects *current* commitment, while price is the *result*. VSA teaches a trader to read that leading data bar by bar, converting "is this move real?" from a guess into an inference from effort/result agreement. It is the most direct way to see professional absorption and distribution, and it elevates every other chart method (price action, Wyckoff, SMC) by adding the confirmation dimension. A trader fluent in VSA reads not just *what* price did, but *whether real money was behind it*.
