# Fibonacci Trading

Fibonacci trading applies the ratios derived from the Fibonacci sequence (0.236, 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786, 0.886, and extensions 1.272, 1.618, 2.618) to price structure to forecast retracement depths, projection targets, and time zones. The premise — disputed but widely practiced — is that crowd behavior produces retracements and extensions that cluster around these ratios with a frequency beyond chance, making them self-fulfilling zones of support/resistance and natural targets.

Fibonacci is less a standalone system than a *measurement framework* grafted onto other methods: it quantifies *how deep* a pullback should go (retracement) and *how far* an impulse should extend (projection). It is the connective tissue of [Elliott Wave Theory](elliott-wave) and [Harmonic Patterns](harmonic-patterns), and a staple confluence tool in [Price Action](price-action) and [Supply & Demand](supply-demand) trading.

## Core Philosophy — Ratios as Natural Harmony

The Fibonacci sequence (1,1,2,3,5,8,13,21...) has the property that consecutive terms converge to the ratio φ ≈ 0.618 (and its inverse 1.618). These ratios and their derivatives appear throughout nature and, the claim goes, in market structure: corrections retrace ~61.8% of an impulse more often than random; impulses extend to 1.618× their first leg. Whether this is genuine self-organization (crowds oscillate at natural frequencies), self-fulfilling prophecy (everyone watches the same levels), or confirmation bias (the literature is skeptical) is debated — but the levels are watched by enough participants to matter as *reaction zones* regardless of cause.

The pragmatic stance: Fibonacci levels are where many traders place orders and stops, so they function as real liquidity magnets. Trade them as *zones of interest requiring confirmation*, not as guaranteed reversal points.

## Fibonacci Retracements

The foundational tool. Given a significant swing low-to-high (uptrend), draw retracement levels measuring *down from the high*:
- 23.6% — shallow retrace; strong trend, shallow pullback.
- 38.2% — common first retrace in a healthy trend.
- 50% — not a true Fib ratio (it's 0.5) but included by convention; Gann's number, a deep-but-common retrace.
- 61.8% — the "golden ratio"; the most-watched level; deep retrace, often the limit of a healthy correction.
- 78.6% — very deep; often the last defense before the trend invalidates.
- 88.6% — harmonic's "deep" zone.

The trader watches price retrace into these zones and looks for a reaction (candle, structure, [Order Flow](order-flow) absorption) to enter in the trend direction. The 61.8% is the most traded; the 38.2% and 50% are common shallow entries in strong trends.

**Critical**: retracements are drawn on *completed* swings. Drawing on an incomplete move projects invalid levels. The swing must be clearly defined (a significant high-to-low or low-to-high with structure).

## Fibonacci Extensions / Projections

Where retracements measure *how deep* a pullback goes, extensions measure *how far* a new impulse leg will travel — the profit target. Computed by projecting ratios (1.272, 1.618, 2.618) of the first leg from the end of the retracement:
- In an uptrend: measure swing low→high (leg 1), wait for retracement, then project 1.618× leg 1 up from the retracement low.
- 1.618 is the primary extension target; 2.618 and 4.236 for strong moves.

Extensions give the trader *where to take profit* — the answer to "how far can this run?" They are the projection analog of retracements and are heavily used in [Harmonic Patterns](harmonic-patterns) (where specific Fib combinations define each pattern) and [Elliott Wave](elliott-wave) (where wave targets are Fib-projected).

## Fibonacci Confluence & Clusters

The single most important refinement: a Fib level is far stronger when it *confluences* with other levels/tools:
- **Multiple-timeframe confluence** — the 61.8% retracement on the daily aligning with the 38.2% on the 4h creates a cluster; clusters > single levels.
- **Fib + structure** — a 61.8% retrace landing on a prior swing high (now support) or a [Supply & Demand](supply-demand) zone is high-confluence.
- **Fib + moving average** — a retrace to 61.8% coinciding with the 50/200 MA.
- **Fib + Fib** — two overlapping retracement sets (from different swings) producing a tight cluster zone.

Confluence is where Fibonacci stops being magic-number superstition and becomes a structured confluence-mapping system: the *density* of overlapping factors at a price defines its importance.

## Fibonacci Time Zones & Fans (Secondary Tools)

- **Time zones** — vertical lines at Fibonacci intervals (1,2,3,5,8,13...) from a swing, predicting *when* a reversal may occur. Weaker evidence than price retracements; used as a timing overlay.
- **Fans** — trendlines drawn from a swing at Fib angles (38.2°, 50°, 61.8°); dynamic support/resistance in trends. Less commonly used than horizontal retracements.
- **Arcs / spirals** — circular Fib projections; more esoteric, less widely practiced.

## The Fibonacci–Elliott–Harmonic Nexus

Fibonacci is the measurement language underlying two major pattern methods:
- **Elliott Wave** — wave relationships are Fibonacci-ratio-based: wave 3 often extends to 1.618× wave 1; wave C to 1.0× or 1.618× wave A; alternation and equality rules use Fib. See [Elliott Wave Theory](elliott-wave).
- **Harmonic Patterns** — each pattern (Gartley, Bat, Butterfly, Crab) is defined by *specific* Fib ratios between its legs (e.g., Gartley's B retrace = 0.618, D completion = 0.786). Fib is the DNA of harmonics. See [Harmonic Patterns](harmonic-patterns).

This is why Fibonacci is taught as foundational: it's the ruler the pattern methods use.

## Risk Management

- **Entry at the zone, stop beyond it** — if long at 61.8% retrace, stop below 78.6% or below the swing. The Fib level defines invalidation structurally.
- **Targets at extensions** — scale out at 1.272 and 1.618 extensions; trail the rest. Don't hold the whole position for 4.236.
- **R:R from Fib structure** — entry at 61.8%, target at 1.618 extension often yields 2:1+ naturally; if the geometry doesn't offer favorable R:R, skip.
- **Confirmation required** — never blindly buy/sell a Fib level; require a candlestick reversal, structure break, or [Order Flow](order-flow) signal at the level. Fib is the *where*, confirmation is the *when*.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Drawing on the wrong swings** — Fib is only as good as the swing you anchor it to. Subjective swing selection is the biggest source of "Fib doesn't work" complaints.
- **Treating levels as guarantees** — price often *overshoots* or *undershoots* a Fib level; trade the reaction, not the exact tick.
- **Too many Fib sets** — drawing retracements from every swing clutters the chart; everything becomes a "level." Use 1–2 dominant swings.
- **Ignoring the trend** — buying a 61.8% retrace in a downtrend is counter-trend gambling; Fib works best with the prevailing trend.
- **Forgetting 50% isn't Fibonacci** — it's Gann/convention; useful but don't claim Fib mysticism for it.
- **Confirmation bias** — remembering the times 61.8% held and forgetting the times it didn't. Backtest the actual hit rate.
- **Extension over-optimism** — assuming price *will* reach 1.618; it often falls short. Scale out incrementally.

## Relationships to Other Methods

- **Elliott Wave** — Fib ratios define wave relationships; inseparable. See [Elliott Wave Theory](elliott-wave).
- **Harmonic Patterns** — Fib ratios define each pattern's geometry. See [Harmonic Patterns](harmonic-patterns).
- **Price Action** — Fib provides the zones; PA provides the trigger candles. See [Price Action Trading](price-action).
- **Supply & Demand** — Fib retracements of the impulse leg often align with S/D zones; high confluence. See [Supply & Demand Trading](supply-demand).
- **Chart Patterns** — Fib measured moves and pattern measured moves overlap; Fib retracements of pattern breakouts offer re-entry. See [Chart Patterns](chart-patterns).
